ANDROID · GMAIL NOTES · IPHONE

Your notes,
across your phones.

Notes Sync for Android connects directly to the Gmail Notes mailbox used by Apple Notes on iPhone. Your phone handles synchronization; no computer or separate sync server is required.

Preview release · Android 8 and later · Google verification pending

Your accounts

Choose your Google account after installation. Add several profiles and switch without signing out. Each profile has separate notes and synchronization state.

Existing notes first

The app downloads existing mailbox notes before enabling automatic upload. An empty Android installation is treated as a first import, never as a request to delete iPhone notes.

Keep writing offline

Create, edit, search, and restore notes on Android. Local changes wait for a connection. Conflicts let you choose which version to keep.

How Google access is used

Google handles account selection and sign-in on its own screen. Notes Sync requests your verified email to identify your profile, and Gmail access to read and upload note messages and move replaced or deleted notes to Trash. The current IMAP connection requires Google's full mail permission; the implemented synchronization operates on your selected Notes folder and targeted moves to Trash.

Notes go directly between your phone and Google over an encrypted connection. No developer-operated server receives your notes. Read our Privacy Policy for storage, access, retention, and deletion details.

Before you start

On iPhone, enable Notes for the same Google account in Apple account settings. This app works with Gmail-backed notes, not iCloud Notes. Attachments and unsupported formatting are read-only. Background timing depends on Android, Google access, and connectivity.

This is a preview. Real-device Gmail and iPhone compatibility testing is ongoing. Keep an independent copy of important notes; export and backup restore are not yet available.

Support

Contact the developer through the support tracker or the support email on Google's consent screen. Never include passwords, access tokens, or private notes in a report.
